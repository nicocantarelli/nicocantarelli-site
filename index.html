Thesis

Companies hiring someone to build a website usually choose between two bad options.

An agency gives you a team, and with the team comes a project manager, an account lead, and a designer who hands the work to a developer who was not in the room when it was scoped. You pay for the coordination as much as the work, and every decision passes through several people before it reaches the one typing.

A cheap freelancer gives you the opposite problem. You become the project manager. You get what you specified rather than what you needed, because nobody on the other side is thinking about the thing as a whole.

I work as one person who takes a project from the first conversation to production and stays after it launches. I scope it, make the architectural decisions, write the code, deploy it, and fix it when something breaks. Nothing is handed off, so nothing is lost in the handoff.

That used to mean a lower ceiling on what one person could deliver. It does not anymore. The mechanical parts of the work take a fraction of the time they used to, which leaves the judgment, and the judgment was always the part that mattered.
